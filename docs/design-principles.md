# Design principles

Made for children who benefit from visual structure (for example with ADHD or dyslexia), and
refined by watching what actually got used.

1. **One question per glance.** "What now?", "What next?", "What do I bring?" each have one obvious
   place. The current lesson is highlighted, past ones fade out.
2. **Time is visible.** Countdowns ("om 12 min", "3 dagar") instead of only clock times. The morning
   clock turns the whole screen a colour, so the child can tell from across the room how much
   time is left.
3. **Concrete tasks, not vague instructions.** "Borsta tänderna", not "gör dig klar". Max 1 to 3 tasks
   per phase, each tappable to tick off.
4. **No repetition.** The packing list merges items across subjects ("Dator: NO, Engelska") and the
   "Kom ihåg" notes leave out what is already in the packing list. Labels are not doubled.
5. **Consistent colour meaning.** Coral = now, blue = next, purple = tests, green = studying,
   pink = after-school activity, yellow = reminders. Never reuse a colour for something else.
6. **Readable text.** Large base sizes (nothing important under 12 px), short lines, calm font,
   plenty of whitespace, no blinking. Full teacher notes open in a large-text overlay.
7. **Calm by default.** Few colours, soft backgrounds, dark theme in the evening, black at night.
   The screen should not be a source of stimulation.
8. **The right view at the right time.** Today in the morning, tomorrow (with "pack tonight") in the
   afternoon, the clock during the morning rush. It drifts back to that view when left alone.
9. **Teachers' words are the source.** The useful bits (bring a laptop, a quiz on Friday) are already
   written by teachers in the school platform; the dashboard's job is to surface them.

When adding features, ask: does the child need this at a glance, or is it something a parent
wants to know? Parent things belong somewhere else.
